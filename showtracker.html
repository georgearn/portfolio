Show Tracker

One list for movies and series: what's releasing soon, what just dropped, and a quick picker for when I can't decide.

Why it exists

The idea is simple: I wanted one overview of what's coming and what just landed, a way to set a reminder for a specific title instead of hoping I'd remember it, and a search that lets me add anything, movie or series, to a single watchlist. From there, a quick picker takes the decision fatigue off my hands: give it a mood, a runtime window, a genre or two, and it either draws from what's already on the list or hands back something fully random.

Metadata comes from two APIs stitched together: TMDB for search, trending titles, synopses, cast, posters, and region-aware "where to watch" data, and OMDb, queried by IMDb ID, for the IMDb rating and Rotten Tomatoes score TMDB doesn't carry itself. Neither IMDb nor Rotten Tomatoes exposes a public API of their own, so this pairing is the practical ceiling on what a hobby project can pull in without scraping.

Built entirely in the cloud

FitApp and Subscription Tracker both began in Python. Show Tracker skipped that step and went straight to native Kotlin and Jetpack Compose, since the toolchain from the Subscription Tracker rebuild was already familiar. There's no Android Studio, no local Gradle, no offline machine involved. Every commit goes to GitHub, and GitHub Actions does the rest.

Push to main

A small workflow under .github/workflows/ triggers on every push.

What it does

Releasing Soon and Just Dropped

Home surfaces two rails: upcoming titles with a bell to arm a notification, and what's newly out with a one-tap add to the watchlist. Both are filtered by preferred genres and country when those are set.

Search and a single watchlist

Discover searches movies and series together against TMDB. Anything found goes onto one list, which doubles as a simple watch log: checked off once seen, deleted once done.

A quick picker for indecision

For You runs a short quiz (mood, movie or series, runtime, genres), then pulls a match from the watchlist. Surprise Me ignores all of it for a fully random pick.

Release-day notifications

No local alarms. A WorkManager job re-checks TMDB for every upcoming title on the watchlist roughly twice a day, and fires a notification the moment its status flips to released.

Genre, region and content filters

Preferred genres filter Home and Just Dropped. A region code decides which streaming services show under "where to watch," and per-country filters hide titles you'd rather not see, with exceptions.

Where TMDB and OMDb fall short

Both APIs are rate-limited and occasionally miss a title, especially smaller or very recent releases. OMDb's ratings depend on TMDB having a matching IMDb ID, so some titles show ratings as unavailable rather than wrong.

Not wired in yet

Notifications track a title as a whole, not a season. A series already marked released doesn't get a second alert when a new season lands. Doing that properly means tracking season-level release dates per series, which is the next piece to design in rather than bolt on.
